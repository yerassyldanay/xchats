Our assistant replies to customers in WhatsApp: prices, stock, delivery. Every
reply is generated by a language model, and the market has dozens of them — fast
and slow, cheap and expensive, accurate and not so accurate. We ran the main
candidates through our own test suite and compared them on three things: what it
costs, how fast the model answers, and how accurate the answer is. Here's what we
measured, and which model we picked.

## Our pick

**We picked `google/gemini-3.5-flash`.** The best accuracy of everything we tested:
**92–97% correct**, depending on how many products are in the catalog. It answers
fast — about 2 seconds. It isn't cheap: **$9–16 per 1,000 answers**, roughly 5x the
next option. We accepted that price for the quality.

A cheaper option if volume grows: `google/gemini-2.5-flash` — $1.80–3.10 per 1,000
answers, 1.2 seconds, but only 82–89% accurate. That means roughly 9 answers out of
every 100 will contain a mistake.

## Honestly: this pick isn't live yet

Before the numbers, two facts that matter more than the table below.

1. **Production is not running the model we recommend.** The model actually
   serving customers today scored **55%** in our tests — the second-worst result
   of everything we measured. Switching to our pick isn't a one-line config edit:
   it means changing both the model itself and its settings (see the prompt note
   below).
2. **The prompt behind these numbers isn't live either.** We tested a third
   version of our prompt — with a clearer response contract and an explicit
   instruction about stock status. Production still runs an older version. The
   accuracy gain from the new prompt (see below) isn't reaching customers yet.

We're sharing this because we believe an honest look at our own weak spots is more
useful than a polished report. Neither fact above gets fixed by publishing this
post — someone still has to ship the change.

## Who we compared

Source: two internal test runs, both on the same (third) version of our prompt.

**On equal footing — all three models ran the same 76 questions (10- and
50-product catalogs):**

| Model | Accuracy | Typical speed | Worst case | Cost per 1,000 answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gemini-3.5-flash | **95%** (72/76) | 2.0s | 2.6s | $9.48 – $12.12 |
| deepseek-v3.2-exp | 91% (69/76) | 11s | **68s** | $1.63 – $2.21 |
| gemini-2.5-flash | 89% (68/76) | 1.2s | 2.3s | $1.84 – $2.39 |

**Only the two Gemini models were tested on the 100-product catalog** — deepseek
was left out of that run, so it has no 100-product score, and its 91% above
shouldn't be compared directly to a 114-question Gemini number:

| Model | Accuracy at 100 products | Cost per 1,000 answers |
|---|---|---|
| gemini-3.5-flash | **97%** (37/38) | $15.82 |
| gemini-2.5-flash | 82% (31/38) | $3.14 |

**On the size gap:** 3.5-flash led at every catalog size we tried — by 8, 7, and 15
points. But all of these tests reuse the same **38 questions**, just asked again
at each catalog size, so 114 rows isn't 114 independent tries. Read the lead as
consistent, not as a precise measurement. The honest summary: 3.5-flash wins, and
its margin grows as the catalog grows.

**Why the cost swings so much:** a bigger catalog means a longer prompt. Tokens
per answer go from 5.5k to 7.4k to 9.9k at 10 → 50 → 100 products, and the prompt
is about 99% of the token count. Cost tracks catalog size almost as strongly as it
tracks which model you pick.

**Deepseek is a trap, not a bargain.** Cheapest per answer, and accuracy close to
3.5-flash — but 11 seconds typical and 68 seconds worst case. A customer won't
wait a full minute for a reply. Deepseek alone made one of our test runs take 3
minutes 38 seconds instead of about 44 seconds — 80% of all request time in that
run.

## Models we tested and ruled out

Not candidates today — kept here so we don't accidentally re-test them. These
numbers come from earlier runs the latest tests didn't repeat:

| Model | Accuracy | Why we ruled it out |
|---|---|---|
| gemini-2.5-flash-lite | 66% | too many mistakes |
| gpt-4o-mini | 55% | too many mistakes — **and this is what production runs today** |
| claude-haiku-4.5 | 74% | slow (4.7s) and expensive |
| minimax-m2.5 | 68% | slow (8.6s) |
| kimi-k2.5 | 77% | too expensive for the result |
| kimi-k2.6 | 52% | every reply cut off mid-thought; 60s |

The bottom four were tested on an older version of our prompt, so their scores
aren't directly comparable to the table above. We ruled them out on speed, price,
or broken output — not on a narrow accuracy gap.

## Approach beats model choice

Three times, changing *how we ask* moved the result more than changing the model:

- **A prompt update, same models, same questions:** deepseek 80% → 87%,
  gemini-2.5-flash 67% → **79%**. Twelve points, no extra cost.
- **One settings line saved 3.5-flash from being dropped.** On default settings it
  scored 65% accuracy at 9.2 seconds, and we almost ruled it out. Turning on a
  minimal reasoning mode (`reasoning: effort: minimal, exclude: true`) took it to
  95%+ at 2.0 seconds. **Our pick only works with that setting — turning on
  3.5-flash without it ships the 65% version.**
- **A bigger catalog hurts the cheap model more than the expensive one.**
  gemini-2.5-flash goes 89% → 89% → 82% at 10/50/100 products; gemini-3.5-flash
  holds 97% → 92% → 97%.

One more note — not about a model or a prompt, but about our own testing: one run
first reported 3 correct answers out of 217. Same replies — after we fixed a bug
in our own scoring code, the same responses scored 112, then 148. Check your test
harness before blaming the model for a bad score.

## What to trust less here

- Costs are **estimates**, priced from numbers we typed into a price table by
  hand and last checked in mid-July. Before relying on these figures for a budget
  decision, re-check the current price of 3.5-flash — it's our pick and the
  expensive one.
- Some repeat runs just replay a saved answer instead of asking the model again:
  $0 and a fraction of a second, not real numbers. One of our runs reused 152 of
  228 answers this way.
- Each catalog size is only 38 questions. Treat a gap smaller than about 8 points
  as noise, not a real difference.
- Kazakh test messages are still a draft, pending review by a native speaker.

## When we'll revisit this

- a listed price moves more than 20%;
- a new model generation ships (the next Gemini, DeepSeek, GPT-mini class, etc.);
- the catalog grows past 100 products — the largest size we've tested, and where
  the cheap model already starts to struggle;
- the prompt changes again — every number in this post is tied to this specific
  version of it;
- 3.5-flash's minimal-reasoning setting stops working, or the provider changes
  its defaults.
